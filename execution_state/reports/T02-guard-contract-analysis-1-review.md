# T02 diagnostic guard analysis — independent review

Date: 2026-10-11

## Verdict

**CHANGES_REQUIRED for the analysis report before its conditional example is used to select a guard.** The arithmetic and the central stop decision are sound: the schema bounds do not select settings, the canonical config remains uncalibrated, and a guard-only object must not be passed off as a measured `PhysicsCalibration`. A separate hashed diagnostic envelope could be safe in principle after a coordinator-owned contract/config amendment, with `physicsCalibration` still null and production/playable runners unable to consume that envelope.

The report needs one narrow physical clarification. Its use of “continuous support” must mean that bodies stay within the proven support volume and cannot fall below their minimum rest elevation, not that they remain in physical contact with the floor throughout a shot. Literal contact throughout would exclude the intended airborne button motion. A horizontal apron alone does not prove that vertical invariant or prevent a body from tunnelling below the slab. Until the invariant is stated and enforced at every substep, the sample guard figures are not ready to select or hash.

## Evidence and arithmetic

- `packages/game-protocol/src/config.ts:60` has `physicsCalibration: null`. `types.ts:342-368,417` and `schemas.ts:33-64,754-788` define one strict calibration object containing coefficients as well as support, workload, and tunnelling fields. The schema bounds validate submitted values; they do not populate a value or select a hashable guard while the field is null. This agrees with `CONTRACTS.md:129-135`, `CONTRACT_WORK.md:7-13,36-82`, and `DECISIONS.md:8,20`.
- The report correctly refuses to set a guard by filling the calibration object with provisional or fabricated coefficients. A separate diagnostic-only object can avoid implying friction/damping calibration only if the coordinator adds it to the canonical typed/schema config and hash, updates fixture hashes, defines the config/schema versioning, restricts its exact diagnostic input domain, and keeps it inaccessible to normal local, AI, online, and deployed play. Neither the current schema nor a worker-private type supplies that contract.
- For the exact D01 center impulse of magnitude 8 on mass 1, `E = J²/(2m) = 32`. The solid-cylinder minimum principal inertias, farthest radii, and resulting speed bounds in the report check out to the displayed precision: cap `Imin=0.0268`, `rMax≈0.3255764`, `vBound=8`, `omegaBound≈48.8678`; button `Imin=0.0006590625`, `rMax≈0.1319327`, `vBound≈20.6559`, `omegaBound≈311.621`. Using the whole energy budget independently for each body is conservative, provided the stated mass-property and no-solver-energy assumptions are verified.
- The `Nrequired` calculation also checks out from the report's provisional example: translation limit `0.01125` (one quarter of the `0.045` button thickness), rotation limit `pi/8`, and budget 120 yield translation count 46, rotation count 7, and a maximum of 46 substeps. `1/(120×46)≈0.0001811594 s` per actual Havok call. The displayed rounded sweep values produce the same ceilings; implementation must derive ceilings from full-precision values and verify actual Havok inertias and observed energy rather than treating the illustrative roundings as selected measurements.
- The apron example is arithmetically consistent: `20.6559×15 + 0.131933≈309.9704`, rounded up to 311, below the schema maximum 10,000. This uses the total-speed bound as a conservative horizontal-speed bound and includes the button's farthest collider radius. It is limited to the one stated impulse domain, a 15-second duration, and every starting point on the painted pitch. It neither adds a wall nor proves traversal or settling.
- The workload arithmetic is correct: 1,800 nominal 120-Hz ticks times 46 substeps is 82,800 actual `executeStep` calls per maximum-duration trial. The report properly does not infer that this fits 30 seconds. Its distinction between `maxBatchSteps` as nominal ticks versus actual Havok calls is necessary; the schema and current config do not settle that meaning.

## Required physical clarification

The fixture starts each upright cap at `y=0.06` and the flat button at `y=0.0225`, matching their minimum center-of-mass elevations above `surfaceY=0`. Under ideal rigid-body energy accounting, those are useful potential-energy reference heights: a body may become airborne or be lofted, but it cannot gain extra kinetic energy from gravity if its full collider remains supported by the slab and cannot pass below its original minimum rest elevation. That is different from continuous contact with the slab.

`worldFactory.ts:211-218` currently builds a finite slab whose top is `surfaceY`; it does not build the proposed apron or instrument a vertical support invariant. The analysis at `T02-guard-contract-analysis-1.md:21,36,40` labels its assumptions, but its phrases “supported bodies throughout” and “continuous support” can be read as floor contact, while the horizontal apron calculation only bounds x/z coverage. The follow-on coordinator contract should instead state and enforce all of the following: the horizontal apron contains the full collider throughout each substep; no collider penetrates below the corresponding minimum rest elevation; actual substep motion/contacts are recorded; and any loss of that support proof, energy growth beyond the envelope, or budget exhaustion returns `resourceLimit`. If that vertical invariant cannot be established, include the maximum allowed gravitational potential drop in `Emax` and recompute velocity, angular, apron, and workload bounds. Do not block ordinary loft by requiring continuous floor contact.

The present ideal energy envelope also assumes the exact center application point, an at-rest canonical start, no other external impulses, restitution no greater than one, nonnegative friction, verified body mass properties, and no solver-created energy. It cannot certify other impulses, off-center torque, restart shots, a different initial state, or a candidate's collision-free button traversal. C00 explicitly requires actual substep instrumentation and whole-button swept-clearance/contact proof; endpoint samples and a positive apex do not satisfy those gates.
